Wall and Drywall Water Damage in Riverview

Drywall acts like a wick. Paper and gypsum pull water up from the floor line, so the wet zone frequently ends a foot or more past the highest point the water itself touched. From the room, the surface may seem dry and sound even though the hidden face and the insulation are saturated. For that reason our team relies on moisture meter readings rather than appearance.

Plenty of walls that get wet can stay. Drywall that got wet with clean water and is still firm can often be dried in place, sometimes by removing the baseboard and moving air through small holes into the cavity. If the drywall has gone soft, crumbles, shows mold or took in dirty water, we do a flood cut, meaning a level cut over the damaged area with all material beneath it removed.

What It Looks Like

  • Nail or screw heads popping out and seams showing through the paint
  • Caulk lines cracking or gaps opening where trim meets the wall
  • One wall noticeably cooler under your hand than the rest
  • Wall coverings lifting, blistering or creasing close to floor level
  • A tide line or discoloration running along the bottom of the wall
  • A musty smell that is stronger near one wall or outlet

What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Mold Inside Cavities

Mold thrives on the rear face of drywall because it is paper covered, dark and has no moving air. It can cover the hidden side while the painted face looks clean.

Walls You Cannot See

Wet drywall behind cabinets, vanities and built-ins gets no airflow and is easy to overlook. We take readings through toe kicks and from the opposite side to find it.

Electrical Hazards Low Down

Receptacles and cables sit low in the wall, right where water collects. Until that circuit is inspected, avoid plugging anything into the affected wall.

Soaked Batts in the Wall

Batts of fiberglass absorb water and keep it pressed to the framing. Drying from inside the room is not enough when the insulation is soaked.

The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

The first few hours

Wicking Begins

Moisture enters where the panel meets the floor and wicks upward from there. Baseboards and the bottom plate take on moisture.

Hours 24 to 48

Swelling and Blisters

The wet line rises higher, baseboards swell and paint begins to blister. Growth is able to get going on the paper that lines the hidden side.

Day 3 to 7

Drywall Breaks Down

Gypsum softens and the paper lets go. A musty odor grows stronger while the batts settle and mold covers more of the unseen face.

Past one week

Damage Reaches the Studs

Studs and plates stay wet long enough to grow mold and begin to decay. A far larger section of wall needs tearing out and rebuilding.

What We Do

Our Wall and Drywall Water Damage Process

1

Map the Moisture

Using meter readings and thermal images, our team outlines where the water went, upward and sideways, on both faces of the wall and in neighboring rooms.

2

Sort Save From Remove

Our call on each section, dry it or take it out, depends on water category, exposure time and the condition of the panel, and we explain it in plain terms.

3

Open Up or Air Out

Walls we are keeping have their baseboards removed so air can be pushed into the cavity. Walls that cannot be kept get a flood cut along a level line, with the wet insulation pulled.

4

Drying and Reconstruction

Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on, usually 3 to 5 days, until the studs and the drywall left in place test dry. The rebuild follows with fresh insulation, drywall installation and finishing, trim and a coat of paint.
