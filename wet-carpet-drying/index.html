Wet Carpet Drying Help in Riverview

There is always more water in a soaked carpet than the surface suggests. The face fibers can feel only damp while the pad underneath is soaked like a sponge and the subfloor below it is wet too. Towels and a shop vacuum reach the top layer and little else. For Riverview homes, our crew handles the extraction, meters the carpet, pad and subfloor, and runs drying gear that gets to the bottom layer.

Whether the carpet can be saved depends on two things, how clean the water was and how long it has been wet. If the water came out of a clean supply line and we reach it fast, the carpet can normally stay down while it dries. Water from an appliance counts as gray, so the pad is frequently removed while the carpet is cleaned and treated. After sewage or flood water from outdoors, neither the carpet nor the pad stays.

Warning Signs

  • Carpet that squishes underfoot or leaves your socks damp when you walk on it
  • Paint blistering near the floor, or baseboard trim that has puffed up
  • Brown or yellow stains wicking up to the tips of the fibers as it dries
  • Backing that separates from the carpet when you lift a corner to look
  • Days after the water was cleaned up, the carpet is cool and clammy to the touch
  • Musty, sour odor that builds whenever the door and windows stay shut
What We Do

Our Wet Carpet Drying Process

1

Find the Edges

Thermal imaging and meter readings show us where the water went, closets and the space under walls included. Then we determine which category of water it is, as that tells us what is safe to keep.

2

Extract the Water

We use weighted extraction tools that squeeze water from carpet and pad at once. A wand by itself leaves much more behind, so this step cuts the drying time afterward.

3

Dry All the Layers

The carpet is either pulled back so the pad can go, or left down with air blown beneath it, whichever the job needs. Air movers and dehumidifiers run until carpet and subfloor both read dry.

4

Clean and Reset

We treat the carpet and subfloor with an antimicrobial where needed, lay new pad if the old one came out, restretch the carpet onto the tack strip, and clean it.

What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Stains From Furniture

Furniture legs sitting on wet carpet release wood stain and rust into the fibers. The furniture also draws up water and swells, and the marks it leaves often never come out.

Mold on the Backing

The dark, damp underside of the carpet is where mold usually begins. You smell it long before you ever see it on top.

Subfloor Damage

Subfloors made of OSB or particleboard puff up and fall apart if they remain wet. The soft spots, squeaking and lifted seams appear weeks afterward, when nobody remembers the spill.

Clean Water Turns Dirty

A clean water spill does not remain clean for long. As it sits in carpet and pad it picks up soil and bacteria, so a carpet that could have been dried may have to be removed.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

First hours

Water Moves Outward

Under the surface, the pad pulls water outward, well beyond what looks wet. It moves under walls and into the next room, and the tack strip starts soaking it up.

Hours 24 to 48

Smell and Mold Start

The damp pad breeds bacteria, and the sour smell of wet carpet follows. Dust held down in the fibers and the carpet backing both give mold a place to begin.

Days 3 through 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

The latex that holds the backing together breaks down, so the carpet delaminates and ripples. Rust forms on the tack strip nails while the subfloor underneath swells.

Past one week

Past Saving

Carpet and pad left wet this long generally cannot stay. The work gets bigger, since mold is often on the subfloor and the lower edge of the drywall.
